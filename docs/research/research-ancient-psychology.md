# As-Sa'ee: Cross-cutting foundation research
Psychology of behaviour + how older civilizations lived, learned and worked + contemplative traditions -> design principles.

Evidence labels: **STRONG** (multiple RCTs or meta-analyses, replicated) / **MODERATE** (meta-analysis with small effects, or solid but limited studies) / **WEAK** (few or low-quality studies, failed replications) / **TRAD** (traditional or textual claim only, not measured).

## 1. Behavioural psychology the app should rest on

| Principle | What the research says | Strength | What it means for the app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habit formation takes months, and a missed day is fine | Lally et al. 2010: 96 people, daily behaviour, time to reach automaticity ranged 18-254 days, **median 66**. Exercise took longer than simple eating/drinking habits. **One missed day did not measurably derail the curve.** [paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/32898894_How_are_habits_formed_Modeling_habit_formation_in_the_real_world), [BPS summary](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit) | MODERATE (one well-designed study, small n; widely misquoted as "21 days") | Use timeframes of 40-90 days rather than 7-day challenges. A miss should never cost the user their history. |
| Implementation intentions ("If X, then I do Y") | Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 meta-analysis: 94 studies, about 8,000 people, **d = 0.65** on goal attainment. [summary](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/10973) | STRONG | Each habit or wird is stored as cue -> action (e.g. "after Fajr -> 1 page hifz"). Anchor to prayers, not to clock times. |
| Mental contrasting + if-then (WOOP) | Meta-analysis shows a small-to-moderate benefit for goal attainment. [Wang et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full) | MODERATE | When setting a goal, ask "what is the main obstacle inside you, and what will you do when it shows up?" |
| Self-monitoring | Harkin et al. 2016 meta-analysis: monitoring progress improves goal attainment (about d = 0.40), and more so when progress is recorded or shared. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025) | STRONG | Logging is good, but logging should feed reflection, not scores. |
| Identity-based change | Bryan et al. 2011 found that "be a voter" raised turnout more than "vote". [PNAS](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/07/Bryan-et-al.-Voter-Published.pdf). **Large replications failed** ([Gerber 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1513727113)), and a [2024 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0261927x241234845) found no effect from noun wording. | WEAK as a wording trick. Identity as values and self-concept is plausible, but evidence is indirect. | Use identity framing (e.g. "a person of Quran") as meaning, not as a manipulation lever. Don't expect copywriting to change behaviour. |
| Self-determination theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness) | Decades of research. The METUX model applies it to technology and says design should satisfy these three needs, not frustrate them. [Peters, Calvo, Ryan 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00797/full) | STRONG (theory), MODERATE (applied to apps) | User authors their own plan (autonomy). Show mastery, not points (competence). Include a teacher, companion or circle (relatedness). |
| Overjustification: rewards can crowd out intrinsic motivation | Deci, Koestner, Ryan 1999: 128 studies. Expected tangible rewards undermine free-choice intrinsic motivation (d about -0.28 to -0.40). Informational, unexpected verbal feedback does not. [PDF](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf) | STRONG (debated by Cameron & Pierce, but robust for expected tangible rewards) | No points, coins or badges for worship or reading. Feedback should be informational ("you've revised Juz 30 four times this month"). For an Islamic practice there's an extra issue: an external reward can compete with ikhlas (sincerity). |
| Gamification | Hamari et al. 2014: positive effects are context-dependent, often fade, and mostly raise *extrinsic* motivation. [review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256743509_Does_Gamification_Work_-_A_Literature_Review_of_Empirical_Studies_on_Gamification), [GitHub natural experiment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.02371) | MODERATE | Borrow structure (levels of mastery, quests as curricula), not slot-machine elements. |
| Broken streaks demotivate | Silverman & Barasch 2023 (JCR, 7 studies): highlighting a *broken* streak lowers later engagement compared with highlighting an intact one. Users treat the streak as a goal in itself. [paper](https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer) | MODERATE | Don't make consecutive-day streaks the main metric. Show "days practised in last 30/40" or a consistency band. |
| "What-the-hell" / abstinence-violation effect | Polivy & Herman: after a lapse, dieters overeat because "the day is ruined". The same pattern appears in addiction relapse. [overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/abstinence-violation) | MODERATE | Never show a day as "failed". Design for partial credit and "minimum viable wird" (Section 4). |
| Self-forgiveness reduces future procrastination | Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett 2010: students who forgave themselves for procrastinating before exam 1 procrastinated less before exam 2. Lower negative mood explained the link. [study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222302476_I_forgive_myself_now_I_can_study_How_self-forgiveness_for_procrastinating_can_reduce_future_procrastination), [self-compassion](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/Procrastination.pdf) | MODERATE | Tone of all copy after a lapse: tawba-style return, never guilt. |
| Fresh-start effect | Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014: aspirational behaviour (gym visits, "diet" searches, goal commitments) spikes after temporal landmarks such as a new week, month, birthday or holiday. [paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901) | MODERATE-STRONG | Offer restarts at *meaningful* landmarks: Jumu'ah, new Hijri month, Ramadan, Dhul-Hijjah's first ten days, a birthday, a move to Spain. |
| Attention residue | Leroy 2009: switching away from an unfinished task leaves residue that impairs the next task. [paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/46489122_Why_is_it_so_Hard_to_do_My_Work_The_Challenge_of_Attention_Residue_when_Switching_Between_Work_Tasks) | MODERATE | Single-task modes. A "closing note" when leaving a session ("where I stopped / next step") to release residue. |
| Notifications fragment attention | Kushlev, Proulx, Dunn 2016 (n=221, within-subject): alerts on led to more inattention and hyperactivity symptoms. [summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/05/160509191843.htm) | MODERATE | Very few notifications, user-chosen, ideally tied to prayer or adhan times. No re-engagement nagging. |
| Retrieval and spacing beat rereading | Dunlosky et al. 2013: practice testing and distributed practice are rated **high utility**. Highlighting, rereading and summarising are **low utility**. [PDF](https://www.whz.de/fileadmin/lehre/hochschuldidaktik/docs/dunloskiimprovingstudentlearning.pdf) | STRONG | Highlights must come back as questions or recall prompts. Spaced review for hifz, Spanish and DevOps notes. |
| Paper vs screen | Delgado et al. 2018 meta-analysis: paper beats screen for comprehension of **expository** text, not narrative text. The gap is larger under time pressure. [PDF](https://www.uv.es/lasalgon/papers/Delgado%202018%20dont%20throw%20away%20your%20printed%20books.pdf) | MODERATE | The reader should slow the user down for study texts (no speed metrics, a pause-and-recall prompt per section). |
| Sleep and circadian rhythm | Adults need about 7 h or more ([AASM consensus](https://jcsm.aasm.org/doi/10.5664/jcsm.4758)). Morning light advances the body clock, and a mismatch between social and body clocks ("social jetlag") is linked to poorer health. [Roenneberg et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22578422/), [review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-39011-x) | STRONG (sleep need), MODERATE (social jetlag effects) | Plan sleep backwards from Fajr. Morning-light and outdoor prompts. Warn when the plan cuts sleep below 7 h. |
| Deliberate practice | It matters, but explains less variance than once claimed (Macnamara et al. 2014 meta-analysis: about 12% across domains). [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810) | MODERATE | Mastery levels should reward *quality of practice and feedback*, not hours logged. |
| Dark patterns and engagement loops | Infinite feeds, variable-ratio rewards, removing stopping points, and loss-framed nags are documented manipulation techniques. [Weizenbaum review](https://ojs.weizenbaum-institut.de/index.php/wjds/article/view/5_3_2/189), [overview](https://uxmag.com/articles/designing-for-dependence-when-ux-turns-tools-into-traps) | MODERATE (mechanism well known, harm effects still being studied) | See the "Avoid" list (Section 5). |

**How to avoid building another addictive or guilt-inducing app (summary):** measure success by *time well spent and then leaving*, not by daily active minutes. Have natural stopping points. Use informational feedback, not rewards. Treat lapses as normal and make returning easy. Keep the user in charge of the plan. Keep notifications rare and chosen by the user. Show nothing comparative or public by default.

## 2. How older civilizations structured life, learning and work

**Islamic golden age**
- **Bayt al-Hikma (Baghdad, 8th-9th c.)**: a translation and copying centre under Harun al-Rashid and al-Ma'mun. Greek, Syriac, Persian and Sanskrit works were translated (Hunayn ibn Ishaq, al-Kindi). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayt_al-hikmah), [Muslim Heritage](http://muslimheritage.com/house-of-wisdom/). *Caveat:* historians such as D. Gutas argue it was mainly a palace library and archive, not a "university". Its romanticised image is partly modern. Lesson: learning was **collaborative, multilingual and built on translation and commentary**. TRAD/historical.
- **Rihla / talab al-'ilm and the ijazah**: students memorised Qur'an young, then travelled to sit with masters. They received an **ijazah**, a personal licence to transmit a specific text, with a chain (isnad) back to the author. [Ijazah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijazah), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Islam/Education). The model is mastery verified by a person, text by text, with a lineage.
- **Matn -> sharh -> hashiya**: memorise a compact core text (matn), then study a commentary (sharh), then glosses (hashiya). **Mutala'a** means preparing the text alone before the lesson. **Mudhakara** means reviewing it afterwards with a partner. Historically documented. This matches modern evidence on retrieval and elaboration.
- **Al-Zarnuji, *Ta'lim al-Muta'allim* (c. 1200)**: choose your teacher, subject and study partner carefully. Repeat lessons on a *decreasing schedule* (the text advises repeating yesterday's lesson more times than older ones). Study between Maghrib and Isha and at **sahar** (before Fajr). Memorise in the early morning. Sincerity, perseverance and light eating help memory. [Huda et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244016670197), [summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377542791_Islamic_Education_Theory_Al-Zarnuji's_Perspective_in_The_Book_Ta'lim_Al-Muta'allim). The repetition schedule is TRAD, but it **converges with spacing research (STRONG)**.
- **Ibn Khaldun, *Muqaddimah* (1377), malakah**: a skill is "a stable quality resulting from a repeated action until its form has taken final shape". It needs **tadrij** (gradual progression), repetition at close intervals, concrete examples, and discussion or debate (muhawara/munazara). He warns against harsh teaching and against overloading learners with too many books or abridgements. [Muslim Heritage](https://muslimheritage.com/ibn-khalduns-education-muqaddima/), [malakah paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398984243_Ibn_Khaldun's_Concept_of_Malakah_and_the_Philosophy_of_Deep_Learning_A_Mindful_Meaningful_and_Joyful_Learning_Perspective). TRAD, but consistent with habit and skill research.
- **Al-Ghazali, *Ihya* Book 10 (Tartib al-Awrad)**: a **wird** is a *portion* of the day or night given to a set practice. He divides the day into about 7 portions (and the night into 5). The awrad fit around work, study, family and sleep, and change with a person's role: scholar, student, worker, ruler, or worshipper. [Fons Vitae](https://fonsvitae.com/product/al-ghazali-the-arrangement-of-the-litanies-and-the-exposition-of-the-night-vigil-book-10/), [ghazali.org PDF](http://www.ghazali.org/books/ihya%20Vol1%20Book10%20part%20Two..pdf). This is directly a **role-aware day template**. TRAD.
- **Ibn Sina, *Canon*: regimen (tadbir)**: health is maintained through six essential factors: air, food and drink, **sleep and wakefulness**, **movement and rest (exercise)**, retention and evacuation, and **states of the soul (emotions)**. He wrote seasonal adjustments to sleep and activity. [non-naturals](https://juliamartins.co.uk/what-were-the-non-naturals), [seasonal sleep, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3745764/), [sleep physiology, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12451318/). The framework is TRAD. The modern equivalents (sleep, exercise, stress) are STRONG.
- **Ibn Qayyim, *al-Fawa'id***: a passing thought becomes an idea, then a desire, then resolve, then an action, then a **habit** that is hard to quit. So intervene at the thought stage, and cure a habit "with its opposite". [source](https://abdurrahman.org/2010/02/03/al-fawaid-a-collection-of-wise-sayings-imam-ibn-al-qayyim/). TRAD. This parallels cue -> craving -> response models and the "competing response" in habit-reversal therapy (MODERATE).
- **Early rising (bukur)**: "O Allah, bless my nation in its early mornings" (Tirmidhi 1212, Abu Dawud 2606). The narrator Sakhr sent his trade caravans out early and prospered. [sunnah.com](https://sunnah.com/abudawud/15/130), [explanation](https://www.abuaminaelias.com/dailyhadithonline/2013/07/29/ummah-blessed-morning/). Small but consistent deeds are most beloved (Bukhari, Kitab al-Riqaq). TRAD/religious, aligned with habit research.

**Greek, Roman and Christian**
- **Askesis / spiritual exercises (Hadot)**: philosophy as a *way of life*. Its practices were attention (**prosoche**), meditations (meletai), memorising core maxims, self-mastery, morning preparation (Marcus Aurelius, *Med.* 2.1), and rehearsing hardships in advance (premeditatio malorum). [IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/hadot/), [Davidson intro](https://pages.charlotte.edu/pact/wp-content/uploads/sites/1333/2023/08/Davidson-SpiritualExercisesAncient-1990.pdf). TRAD.
- **Evening review**: Seneca, *De Ira* 3.36, following Sextius, asked at night "What bad habit have you cured today? What fault resisted? In what are you better?" The review was gentle, and he says "how sweet the sleep that follows". [text](https://theosophyproject.blogspot.com/2016/10/senecas-daily-examination-of-conscience.html). This is structurally almost identical to **muhasaba**.
- **Otium / negotium**: for Romans, *otium* was not idleness but cultivated leisure for study and reflection (Seneca *De Otio*, Cicero). *Negotium* ("not-leisure") was business and public duty. Their order of value was the reverse of modern hustle culture. TRAD.
- **Monastic horarium (Rule of Benedict ch. 48)**: "Idleness is the enemy of the soul". The day was split between the Office (about 3 h), manual labour (about 5 h) and **lectio** (about 2-3 h), and the schedule shifted with the season. [RB 48](https://pressbooks.palni.org/ruleofbenedict/chapter/rb-48/), [a monk's day](https://www.conceptionabbey.org/monastery/a-monks-day/). **Lectio divina** was slow reading, often aloud, followed by *ruminatio* (chewing the text), meditation, prayer and rest. [Lectio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lectio_Divina). TRAD.
- **Commonplace books**: from Erasmus's *De Copia* (1512) onwards, readers copied excerpts under subject headings (*loci communes*) to reuse in their own writing and speaking. The image is the bee turning nectar into honey. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-14169-5_223), Ann Blair, *Too Much to Know* ([talk](https://english.yale.edu/node/4076)). TRAD. It is effective when it involves *re-use* (generation, elaboration: MODERATE), not passive collecting (highlighting: low utility).

**Craft, memory and time**
- **Apprenticeship and futuwwa**: the Islamic futuwwa and Anatolian **akhi** brotherhoods joined a trade with an ethic of chivalry (generosity, loyalty, modesty). Members progressed through ranks, apprentice (mubtadi') -> journeyman (sani') -> master (mu'allim), under a guild shaykh. [Futuwwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futuwwa), [Akhiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhiya), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/futuwa), [Bosnian guilds](https://www.academia.edu/31742004/The_Noble_Traders_the_Islamic_Tradition_of_Spiritual_Chivalry_Futuwwa_in_Bosnian_Trade_Guilds_16th_19th_centuries_). The modern "cognitive apprenticeship" model (Collins, Brown & Newman 1989: modelling -> coaching -> scaffolding -> fading) formalises it (MODERATE). This fits DevOps learning especially well.
- **Method of loci / memory palace**: used in classical rhetoric (Cicero, Quintilian). Oral cultures used songlines and landscapes in the same way (Yates, *The Art of Memory*; L. Kelly, *The Memory Code*). Dresler et al. 2017 (*Neuron*): 30 min a day for 40 days more than doubled word-list recall (26 -> 62 of 72), and the gain was still there 4 months later. [Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(17)30087-9), [summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/03/170308131236.htm). STRONG for lists and order. **Note for hifz:** the traditional method is oral repetition with a teacher (sabaq/sabqi/manzil). Loci can help with *order* (which surah or page comes next, mutashabihat), but they don't replace recitation.
- **Solar and seasonal time**: before clocks, the day was set by sunrise, solar noon and sunset (the prayer times themselves), and work hours shifted with the season (RB 48, Ibn Sina's seasonal regimen). **Spain-specific:** Spain runs on CET although its longitude matches GMT. Madrid's solar noon falls around 13:15-14:15 and winter sunrise around 08:30. A clock-based "5 a.m. routine" would therefore drift badly, while a **prayer-anchored** day follows the real sun automatically.

## 3. Mystical and contemplative disciplines: traditional claim vs measured evidence

These are described as practitioners describe them. Sufi practices sit within the shari'a, and the tradition itself insists on a qualified guide. "Measured evidence" refers to the closest secular analogue studied, and that analogue is **not the same thing** as the practice.

| Practice | What it consisted of (traditional) | Intended effect (traditional claim) | Closest measured analogue and evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wird** (daily portion) | A fixed daily set of Qur'an, dhikr and prayers, often assigned by a shaykh. Kept even when small. | Constancy (istiqama), and a heart shaped by repetition. | Habit formation (Lally), implementation intentions: STRONG-MODERATE. Consistency over intensity is well supported. |
| **Dhikr** | Remembrance: repeated formulae (tasbih, tahlil, istighfar), alone or in a gathering, sometimes with breath. | Tranquillity of the heart (Qur'an 13:28), presence with God. | Mantra meditation: low or insufficient evidence in [Goyal 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1809754). Dhikr-specific RCTs report less anxiety and stress ([examples](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397982595_Islamic_Spiritual_Practice_as_a_Pathway_to_Psychological_Well-being_Evaluating_the_Impact_of_Devotional_Remembrance_Dhikr_on_Stress)), but they are small and often low quality: **WEAK-MODERATE**. Its value to a believer doesn't depend on this. |
| **Muraqaba** (watchfulness) | Sustained awareness that God sees you. Later Sufi orders added seated meditation. Named from al-Raqib. Al-Muhasibi (d. 857) systematised it. [Muhasibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Muhasibi) | Ihsan, guarding thoughts at their origin. | Mindfulness meditation: small improvements in anxiety, depression and pain, MODERATE ([Goyal 2014](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/1809754)). |
| **Muhasaba** (self-accounting) | A daily or nightly audit of deeds, words and intentions, followed by istighfar and resolve. The sequence was musharata (setting conditions in the morning) -> muraqaba -> muhasaba. [Muhasaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhasaba), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/muhasabah) | Correcting the self before the Final Reckoning. | Self-monitoring STRONG ([Harkin 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025)). Expressive or reflective writing has a small effect (d about 0.15) ([Frattaroli 2006](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/2006-frattaroli.pdf)). **Risk:** brooding rumination worsens mood ([Watkins 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.163)). It should be short, concrete, and end with a resolve and forgiveness, which is exactly the classical form. |
| **Khalwa / chilla** | A seclusion retreat, classically 40 days, of dhikr, fasting and little sleep, **only under a shaykh**. The tradition warns against doing it alone. [Khalwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalwa_(Sufism)) | Breaking attachments, unveiling. | Intensive retreats have benefits, but [Lindahl et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0176239) documents challenging or adverse experiences. This **agrees with the tradition's warning**. The app should *not* run khalwa. It can offer a gentle "mini-khalwa" (a phone-free hour, i'tikaf reminders). |
| **Suhba** (companionship) | Keeping company with the shaykh and the brethren. Character is learned by proximity. | Transmission of adab and states. | Social support and accountability. Relatedness in SDT is STRONG. Shared progress improves monitoring effects (Harkin). |
| **Shukr** (gratitude) | Gratitude in the heart, on the tongue, and in deeds. | Increase (Qur'an 14:7), contentment. | Gratitude interventions have small effects (happiness g about 0.16; [Dickens 2017](https://researchgate.net/publication/317250176_Using_Gratitude_to_Promote_Positive_Change_A_Series_of_Meta-Analyses_Investigating_the_Effectiveness_of_Gratitude_Interventions)): MODERATE (small). |
| **Comparable traditions** | Stoic evening review and prosoche. Benedictine lectio and horarium. Desert Fathers' watchfulness (nepsis). Buddhist mindfulness (sati). | Similar goals: attention, self-knowledge, ordered time. | These converge: attention training, reflective review and structured time keep reappearing across civilizations. That is weak evidence on its own, but it lines up with the measured findings above. |

## 4. Synthesis: design principles (14)

1. **Anchor the day to the sun and the salah, not the clock.** The five prayers are the scaffold for every block. This is solar-correct for Spain. (Ghazali's awrad, RB 48, bukur hadith, circadian research.)
2. **Consistency over intensity: the small wird.** Every practice has a *minimum* form (1 ayah, 1 page, 5 push-ups) that counts fully. (Hadith on small constant deeds, Lally, what-the-hell effect.)
3. **No failed days, only returns.** Lapses are shown neutrally. Return is one tap and the copy is in the spirit of tawba. History is never reset. (Wohl 2010, Silverman & Barasch 2023.)
4. **Show consistency windows, not streaks.** For example "31 of the last 40 days" or a 40-day chilla arc. 40 is culturally resonant and close to the habit median.
5. **If-then plans, attached to cues the user already has.** "After Fajr, then..." (Gollwitzer, d = 0.65.)
6. **The user writes their own plan, with templates from tradition.** Autonomy comes first. Ghazali-style role templates (student / worker / traveller) are starting points. (SDT.)
7. **Mastery through a licence, not points.** Levels are earned by demonstrated recall or skill, ideally checked by a person (teacher, partner). Each level states *what you can now do*. (Ijazah, futuwwa ranks, Ibn Khaldun's malakah, overjustification research.)
8. **Gradual progression (tadrij).** The app suggests the next small step only once the current one is stable. No overload of many books at once. (Ibn Khaldun.)
9. **Retrieval over rereading in every module.** Highlights, ayat, Spanish words and DevOps notes all come back as spaced questions. (Dunlosky, al-Zarnuji's repetition.)
10. **Morning intention, evening accounting.** Musharata at Fajr (1-3 intentions). Muhasaba after Isha (3 questions, under 3 minutes, ending with istighfar, gratitude and one resolve). Not open-ended journaling, to avoid rumination. (Seneca, al-Muhasibi, Harkin.)
11. **Protect attention.** One thing at a time. Write a closing note before switching. Notifications only if the user opts in, at most a few a day, tied to prayer. No feed anywhere. (Leroy, Kushlev.)
12. **Regimen, not optimisation.** Health is a balance of sleep, movement, food, air/light and emotions, with seasonal adjustment. Sleep is planned backwards from Fajr with a 7 h floor. (Ibn Sina, AASM.)
13. **Companionship is built in.** An optional study partner, teacher or circle, with private sharing to chosen people only. No public leaderboards. (Suhba, mudhakara, SDT relatedness.)
14. **Success means closing the app.** Sessions have a natural end ("Your wird is complete. Go live."). Measure good days, not screen time.

### Features per module
- **Reader:** *Commonplace book* (highlights filed under user-made headings/loci, and resurfaced for re-use when writing or creating content). *Mutala'a mode* (preview the questions before reading, then recall afterwards). *Sharh layer* (your own commentary threaded under a passage, like matn/sharh/hashiya). *Lectio / slow mode* (one passage at a time, a pause, then "what stayed with you?"). Spaced review of highlights as questions. No reading-speed or page-count leaderboard.
- **Planner:** *Sunrise/salah-anchored day* (blocks between prayers, auto-shifted by date and location). *Ghazali role templates*. *Fajr musharata* (set intentions). *Isha muhasaba*. *Otium block* (protected unproductive reflection or rest, valued and not "wasted"). *Weekly Jumu'ah fresh start* and *Hijri-month fresh start*. *Seasonal horarium* (summer/winter variants, as in RB 48).
- **Fitness:** framed as an Ibn Sina *regimen*, with six dials: sleep, movement, food, light/air, emotions, rest. A minimum session that counts fully. Exercise habits take longer than others (Lally), so use a 90-day arc and not a 30-day one. Morning-light and outdoor-walk prompts. Seasonal adjustment.
- **Islamic study:** *Wird builder* (daily portion with a minimum form). *Hifz on sabaq/sabqi/manzil* with spaced repetition. *Memory palace for surah order and mutashabihat* (optional aid, alongside oral recitation). *Ijazah-style levels* with sign-off by a teacher or partner. *Adhkar tied to their natural times* (morning/evening, after salah). *40-day chilla arcs*.
- **Spanish (later):** retrieval-first and spaced. A *rihla framing* (learning for the journey; real-world missions in Spain). Loci for vocabulary sets. A conversation partner (suhba / mudhakara).
- **Content creation (later):** draws from the commonplace book (Erasmus's copia: collect -> arrange -> compose). An apprenticeship ladder: imitate a model, then adapt it, then create your own.
- **DevOps learning:** a *cognitive apprenticeship / futuwwa ladder* (mubtadi -> sani' -> mu'allim) defined by demonstrated capabilities, such as "deployed X, debugged Y", not hours.
- **Personal AI (later):** acts as a *companion and examiner*, not a feed. It quizzes you (retrieval), helps with muhasaba with gentle questions, and suggests the next tadrij step. It must never use engagement optimisation or guilt nudges.

## 5. Modern patterns to avoid
- Infinite or algorithmic feeds, "discover" scroll, autoplay, and pull-to-refresh with variable rewards.
- Points, coins, XP or badges for worship or reading (overjustification, ikhlas). Public leaderboards and social comparison.
- Fragile consecutive-day streaks, "you lost your streak" screens, and paid streak freezes.
- Guilt or loss-framed notifications ("you're falling behind", sad mascots) and re-engagement spam.
- "Productivity maximalism": packing every hour, measuring hours, glorifying hustle, cutting sleep. (The Roman and monastic ideal kept otium and lectio.)
- Quantifying the unquantifiable: "spirituality scores", or ranking worship.
- Speed reading and page-count vanity metrics. Passive highlight hoarding with no resurfacing.
- Endless open-ended journaling prompts that invite rumination.
- 21-day or "7 days to a new you" claims (contradicted by Lally).
- Dark patterns: hidden exits, confirm-shaming, forced sign-ups, manipulative upsells.
